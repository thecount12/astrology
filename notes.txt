Step 1: Establish Universal Time (UT / GMT)

An ephemeris catalogs planetary data uniformly in Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) or Universal Time (UT).

1. Convert the local birth time into a standard 24-hour military clock.
2. Subtract 1 hour if Daylight Saving Time (DST) was active during birth.
3. Apply the Time Zone Offset relative to Greenwich, England. Add hours if the birth location is West of Greenwic    ; subtract hours if East.

Formula: UT = Local Time - DST Adjustment +/- Zone Offset 
+/-: plus or minus

Step 2: Extract the Raw Planetary Data

Using a standard physical ephemeris (such as the Rosicrucian Ephemeris or standard Swiss Ephemeris data tables), locate the data row for the correct calendar date.

* Note down the raw positions for the Sun, Moon, and planets.

* Because these tables record positions at exactly 0:00 (Midnight UT), calculate the precise motion fraction by de  termining what percentage of the 24-hour day (%D) elapsed between midnight and your target birth UT.

* Multiply each planet's daily velocity by \(\%D\) and add (or subtract, if the planet is marked retrograde) that   value to the base midnight position.

Step 3: Apply the Classical Non-Tropical (Sidereal) Correction

An ephemeris uses tropical coordinates based on the moving Equinoxes. To anchor your data to the actual structural constellations, you must calculate and apply a mathematical offset called an Ayanamsha.

The most universally accepted classical standard is the Lahiri Ayanamsha (the official standard adopted by the Indian government). It measures cosmic drift from a fixed baseline year (around 285 CE) when the tropical and sidereal zodiacs perfectly aligned.

1. Look up the specific Ayanamsha value for the birth year. For charts cast in the mid-2020s, this offset is appro   ximately 24°13' to 24°15'

3. Subtract the Ayanamsha value from that absolute degree number.

4. Convert the resulting absolute degree value back into the standard 30° zodiacal format.

Step 4: Calculate the True Ascendant (Rising Sign)

The Ascendant is the intersection point where the eastern horizon crosses the path of the ecliptic at the exact minute of birth.

1. Find the base Sidereal Time (ST) for Midnight UT on your chart's calendar date from your ephemeris tables.

2. Calculate the Local Sidereal Time (LST) by applying this standard conversion formula:

Formula: LST = Base ST + UT Birth Time + (UT Birt Time x 0.002739) +/- Local Longitude Equivalent

(Note: The 0.0027379 term is the acceleration correction factoring in the earth's rotation relative to background stars rather than the Sun).

Why Plan 9's astro and scat Fall Short

* Tropical/Observational Bias: The native astro command is fundamentally built for observational astronomy, produc  ing equatorial (Right Ascension/Declination) or topocentric (Azimuth/Altitude) tracking coordinates. It lacks an  integrated Ayanamsha (sidereal shift calculation) mechanism to mathematically lock positions to fixed background  stellar structures.

* Database Disconnect: The scat command is an interface designed to query deep-space stellar catalogs like the SAO  or NGC and tap into the Digitized Sky Survey. It handles objects outside the solar system, which is total 
  overkill for tracking the 7 traditional classical planets.

* Graphic Limitations: Plan 9's plot utility processes simple vector paths but lacks semantic awareness of 
  typographic alignment, precise text-wrapping inside specific circular sectors, or the structural layout logic of  astrological houses.
